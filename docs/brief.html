1. In One Paragraph

Hospitals in Yugaantar Pradesh merge heterogeneous clinical records across English, Devanagari Hindi, and Hinglish spanning 4 disparate hospital networks into a unified research database. Standard PII scrubbers fail catastrophically: naive redactors destroy essential medical utility (scrubbing blood pressures, drug dosages, and disease eponyms) while leaving patients vulnerable to cross-script transliteration traps, family-shared phones, and quasi-identifier re-identification attacks. Phantom Troupe's RAKSHA operates as an Evidence-Guided Privacy Compiler: it ingests multi-format data safely, applies a role-first clinical veto, constructs a cross-script phonetic entity graph with negative evidence constraints, enforces risk-gated k-anonymity with guaranteed true-age containment, and executes an exhaustive $O(N)$ sweep verifier to guarantee mathematical zero-leak enforcement across all release channels.

3. How Each Component Works

  • Safe Streaming Ingestion: Bounded, memory-conscious parsers resiliently handle CSV, TSV (UTF-16 with BOM), JSON, FHIR R4 bundles, and clinical narratives across UTF-8, UTF-16, and CP1252. Malformed, corrupted, or excessively nested documents (>100 levels) are automatically isolated into quarantine with explicit audit reasons, eliminating decompression bombs and memory exhaustion.
  • Role-First Multilingual Detection: Solves the Meaning Trap by syntactically differentiating clinical complaints from relative names (e.g. C/O fever vs C/O Ramesh, S/o, W/o, D/o) and protecting doctor names (Dr. Sharma). A protected clinical lexicon of 250+ drugs, lab units, and eponyms vetoes false redactions, preserving vital dosages (Dolo 650 BD) and vitals (120/80 mmHg). Unseen headers are classified via semantic fuzzy synonyms and value-fingerprint profiling.
  • Cross-Script Phonetic Entity Linkage: Resolves the Language Trap (Mohd, Mohammad, मोहम्मद) via Devanagari transliteration, matra collapsing, honorific stripping, and phonetic skeletons. Employs a 3-pass evidence graph: (1) deterministic matching on strong national/hospital IDs, (2) phone matching with corroboration guarding against family-shared phones, and (3) fragment margin scoring requiring score ≥ 0.8 and margin ≥ 0.2 over runner-up. Must-not-link constraints strictly forbid merging conflicting IDs, opposing genders, or age gaps > 5 years. Ambiguous records safely remain patient: null.
  • Risk-Gated Privacy Planner: Evaluates $k$-anonymity against external voter registries. When demographic equivalence classes fall below $k=5$, an automated escalation ladder widens 5-year age bands to 10-year brackets (mathematically guaranteed to contain the patient's true age) and generalises village/district locations. Synchronous cluster date shifting preserves hospital-stay duration to the exact day.
  • Security Architecture & Keyed HMAC: RAKSHA_SECRET resides strictly in memory and is never written to outputs or logs. Patient pseudonyms are issued via HMAC-SHA256 in Crockford Base32 (PSN-XXXXXX). Prior to export, an exhaustive $O(N)$ tokenized sweep verifier scans all sanitized content, headers, keys, and filenames against removed PII, raw Aadhaar/phone regexes, canaries, and secret tokens. Any violation immediately routes the file to quarantine.

6. Known Limitations & Design Trade-Offs

  • Linkage Conservatism: When identity evidence is ambiguous (e.g. matching name and village without phone or MRN corroboration), RAKSHA intentionally assigns patient: null. We treat false identity merges in clinical research as significantly worse than unlinked fragments.
  • Quasi-Identifier Generalization: For rare patient profiles ($k < 5$), age bands are broadened to 10 years (e.g. 40-49) and fine locations suppressed to district level, prioritizing privacy over fine-grained demographic analysis.
  • Fail-Safe Quarantine Over Partial Leaks: Corrupted files, recursion depths exceeding 100, or canary-seeded documents are quarantined in their entirety rather than partially exported, ensuring zero untrusted leakage enters the research enclave.
